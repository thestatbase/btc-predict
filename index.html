How to read this: Every minute, the system stores a forecast before it knows the answer. Once time passes, it compares the forecast with the later price. Flat outcomes (moves under one basis point) are shown separately and are not counted as “down.”

Each horizon has its own recent evaluation window. “Brier score” measures probability error: 0 is perfect; 0.250 is a 50/50 baseline. Do not treat a small or short-lived sample as proof of predictive skill.

WHAT IMPROVES — AND WHAT DOES NOT

A bounded learner, not a black box.

The model starts with explicit, horizon-specific statistical weights for 1-minute and 5-minute momentum, Kalman-filtered trend, reversal distance, and GARCH volatility. After a forecast resolves, it makes one small regularized logistic update. Updates are bounded so a short streak cannot radically rewrite the model.

It also calibrates its raw probability using a smoothed record of comparable past probability buckets. This deliberately pulls unreliable or low-sample estimates toward 50%. The audit page distinguishes directional outcomes from nearly flat outcomes and reports the number of examples used.

Inputs are public Coinbase ticker and one-minute candle data. The browser uses Coinbase’s public WebSocket ticker for visual price updates; the persistent Worker uses public REST data for reproducible recorded forecasts. Market data can be delayed or unavailable. This is educational research, not financial, investment, or trading advice.
